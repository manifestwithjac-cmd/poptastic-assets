GARLAND CONSTRUCTION — follow the attached reference photograph for physical construction only. Copy its balloon anatomy, density, size hierarchy, silhouette, latex texture and photographic realism. Do NOT copy its colors, bows, theme, backdrop, room or decorations.

SIZE HIERARCHY — three clearly separate sizes only, no intermediate sizes:
- SMALL: approximately 5 inch, half the diameter of a standard or less
- STANDARD: approximately 10 to 11 inch
- LARGE: approximately 16 to 18 inch, only 1.5 to 1.7 times a standard

STANDARD BALLOONS DOMINATE. Most visible balloons are the same standard 10 to 11 inch size, running consecutively through long stretches so they read as the structural backbone. Use approximately only 4 to 5 visibly oversized balloons in the entire garland, separated by several standard balloons, never adjacent to each other. No 24 inch or 36 inch balloons. No giant balloons at the bottom.

NO PYRAMID BASE. The bottom must not form a wide triangular base and must not look like a column standing on a balloon foundation. It simply begins at floor level and continues organically upward, only slightly wider than nearby sections. No symmetrical foundation, no bottom-heaviness.

MEANDERING PATH. The garland must not climb as a straight vertical column and must have no visible straight central axis. It moves slightly inward, then outward, then inward again as it climbs. Some clusters project outward, some tuck against the backdrop, some spill slightly across its face. It should read as an irregular organic vine following the backdrop.

MINI QUADS. Include approximately 5 to 7 distinct mini clusters, each made of about four tightly grouped 5 inch balloons that read as one tiny four-petal group attached to the garland. Never scatter individual small balloons, never use pairs, never use small balloons as a gradual transition size. Leave substantial stretches with no small balloons at all.

IRREGULAR OUTER EDGE. No smooth continuous contour and no consistent diameter. Individual standard balloons stick outward, shallow valleys open between balloons, the width goes narrow, then fuller, then narrow again. Hand-assembled, not mathematically generated.

REAL LATEX SHAPE. Avoid perfect spheres. Standard balloons vary in width, height, orientation and inflation shape. Some are subtly oval or pear shaped. Some face the camera, some turn sideways, some are partially obscured. No two balloons share identical geometry.

REAL LATEX LIGHT. Critical: do not place the same small white oval highlight on every balloon, this is a CGI artifact. Reflections change with orientation. Some balloons carry a broad soft window reflection, some an extremely subtle highlight, some facing away from the light almost none, some reflect neighboring balloons. Highlights differ in size, position, intensity, shape and softness. Use broad diffused environmental reflections. The surface must look like real latex photographed in natural diffused daylight, not rendered plastic.

DEPTH AND OCCLUSION. Do not show every balloon completely. Create realistic overlaps so some balloons are roughly 30 percent visible, some 50 percent, some 70 percent, some fully visible. Balloons behind the front layer are partially obscured. This prevents the installation from looking like separate circles arranged on a flat surface.

DENSITY. Airy professional density and a relatively narrow overall width. Not overstuffed.
